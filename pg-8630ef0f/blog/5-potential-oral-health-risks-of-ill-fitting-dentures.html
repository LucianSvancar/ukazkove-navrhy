5 Complications of Ill-Fitting Dentures

February 6, 2024

Call (430) 342-1083Request appointment

Learn about the essential role dentures play in restoring bite function and improving your smile as vital replacements for missing teeth. Well-fitted full dentures provide secure adhesion to the gums, while partial dentures blend naturally with your existing teeth. However, poorly fitting dentures can lead to various complications. Keep reading to discover five issues that may arise from ill-fitting dentures.

#1: Trouble Speaking

Poorly fitting dentures can lead to instability, making it difficult to speak clearly. This instability poses communication challenges and raises the risk of denture displacement, which may even contribute to developing a lisp in some cases.

#2: Swollen Gums and Blisters

Ill-fitting dentures can worsen gum swelling, especially if the dental restorations irritate the gums, causing increased pressure and friction that may lead to blisters. Inflamed gums are more prone to injury, and if these blisters break, oral bacteria can enter, greatly raising the risk of gum disease. While temporary inflammation might seem minor, persistent swelling has been associated with various health issues in other parts of the body, such as:

  • Asthma
  • Alzheimer’s disease
  • Rheumatoid arthritis
  • Heart disease
  • Cancer
  • Type 2 diabetes

#3: Trouble Eating

Poorly fitting dentures can cause frequent movement in your mouth, leading to difficulties with biting and chewing, which can be uncomfortable or painful. Moreover, these dentures might trap food between the restorations and gums, creating an environment for oral bacteria to flourish. Consequently, these bacteria produce acids that weaken enamel, making teeth more prone to decay.

#4: Angular Cheilitis

Angular cheilitis occurs due to a bacterial infection triggered by saliva buildup at the corners of the mouth, with uneven bites increasing the risk, especially among those with ill-fitted dentures. Symptoms of angular cheilitis comprise:

  • Irritation or cracks at the corners of the mouth
  • Burning sensation
  • Redness
  • Dry lips
  • Rashes

#5: Damage to Natural Teeth

When dentures become loose and shift in your mouth, they can jeopardize the health of your natural teeth. This movement may create unnatural contact between your dentures and your real teeth, resulting in cracks, chips, and enamel erosion. These problems not only weaken your teeth but also raise the risk of decay.

Don’t delay in addressing problems with poorly fitting dentures. By taking action promptly, you can safeguard the health and durability of your natural teeth. Schedule a consultation with your dentist to address these issues and restore a smile that exudes both beauty and well-being, boosting your confidence and comfort along the way!
