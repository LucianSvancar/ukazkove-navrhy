While your teeth certainly deserve a lot of attention, the condition of your gums matters just as much when it comes to the health of your mouth. Not only that, but people who suffer from periodontal disease (better known as gum disease) have also been shown to have an increased risk of conditions like cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and even dementia. That’s why the team at Pleasant Grove Family Dentistry takes gum disease very seriously and offers multiple types of treatment. To learn a little more about this often overlooked problem, read on below.

What is Gum Disease?

Whether it’s called gum disease or periodontal disease, it refers to a bacterial infection that harms the soft tissues that support the teeth as well as the underlying jawbone. In most cases, a lack of oral hygiene leads to a buildup of bacteria-filled plaque, and this is the underlying cause of the condition. It typically occurs in two distinct stages—gingivitis (mild) and periodontitis (advanced).

Signs of Gum Disease

People with gum disease will typically experience:

  • Red, swollen, and tender gums
  • Persistent bad breath (even after brushing)
  • Gums that bleed often while cleaning
  • A change in how the bite feels
  • Gums that pull away from the teeth
  • Loose teeth

Because gum disease affects the base of the teeth, it can even lead to tooth loss, and it’s actually the leading cause of this for adults around the world.

How Do We Treat Gum Disease?

Fortunately, if you do develop gum disease, there is plenty our team can do about it. We’ll keep an eye on your gums during your regular checkups, and if we notice any of the signs listed above, we’ll recommend one of the following treatments to quickly get your oral health back on track.

Scaling & Root Planing

This pair of treatments is often collectively called a “deep cleaning,” and it largely resembles a traditional dental cleaning. The key difference is that it mostly focuses on the gumline and roots of the teeth. This helps your gums heal quickly, restoring your oral health in the process. To learn more about this service, including how to know if you’d benefit from it, keep reading!

Do I Need Scaling & Root Planing?

At your biannual visits to our office, our dedicated Texarkana dentists, Dr. Marracino and Dr. Cockerell, will closely examine your teeth and gums to ensure any dental problems that develop are caught and treated as early as possible. Between appointments, it’s also important that you keep an eye out for:

  • Bleeding or receding gums
  • A persistent bad taste in your mouth, even after brushing
  • Changes to your bite
  • Difficulty chewing
  • Visible plaque buildup

If these or any other abnormal dental symptoms arise, call us ASAP to schedule an exam.

The Process of Scaling & Root Planing

The first portion of the procedure is scaling, which is when any and all plaque and tartar are gently removed with the special tools we have at our office. Once that’s complete, we will move onto the next portion: root planing. At this point, the roots of the teeth are slightly reshaped and smoothed to help prevent the buildup of plaque and bacteria in the future.

Aftercare Tips for Scaling & Root Planing

Since your mouth will still be numb when you leave our office, we recommend not eating for an hour or two following your appointment. We also recommend rinsing periodically with warm saltwater, committing to a solid oral hygiene regimen (being careful around the treated area, of course), and getting plenty of rest.

Antibiotic Therapy

Over time, small pockets of bacteria can form between the gums and teeth as a result of gum disease, and these are extremely difficult to clean, even with scaling. So, we can apply a topical antibiotic or have the patient use a prescription rinse, which will enable the medication to reach these areas. Not only does this approach help deal with the infection, but it can also shrink the pockets to lower the chances of reinfection.

Soft Tissue Laser

Our soft tissue laser allows us to clean the roots of the teeth using a highly concentrated beam of light. It is extremely precise and emits almost no heat, sound, or vibration, so it is very comfortable from the patient’s perspective. Even better, it actually cauterizes blood vessels and sanitizes the area as it works, which reduces bleeding and post-treatment discomfort to practically zero.

How to Maintain Gum Health

The two pillars of great ongoing gum care consist of daily brushing and flossing at home paired with regular checkups and cleanings at Pleasant Grove Family Dentistry. Your oral hygiene routine will largely prevent the buildup that leads to gum disease, and your visits here will allow a hygienist to clean your trouble spots, plus your dentist can spot any potential infection early so it can be treated right away. It should be noted that if you have any of the gum disease symptoms we touched on earlier, it’s wise to go ahead and schedule an appointment with us so we can prevent the problem from becoming worse.

Gum Disease Treatment FAQs

Is there a way to make gum disease treatment more affordable?

Dental insurance providers often cover a portion of the cost of orthodontic treatment. So, if you are insured, you shouldn’t hesitate to provide us with your membership information! Since we are in-network with Texas BlueCross BlueShield, Connection Dental, and Cigna, we can help you maximize your available benefits and lower your out-of-pocket expenses.

Don’t have dental insurance? Consider signing up for CareCredit! They offer convenient payment plans so you can break up the total cost of your treatment into smaller, more manageable chunks over the course of several months.

How common is gum disease?

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention estimates that 4 in 10 adults over the age of 30 have some form of gum disease. That’s why it’s so important to be aware of risk factors, including poor oral hygiene. You should also keep an eye out for bleeding when you floss, tender gums, and other common warning signs. This increases our chances of being able to intervene before the infection can spread throughout your mouth and body.

Is mouthwash good for gum disease?

Yes. That’s why we recommend rinsing with mouthwash for 30-60 seconds as well as brushing and flossing consistently. If you’re not sure which one to use, don’t hesitate to get in touch with our Texarkana dental team! We’d be happy to answer any questions you have about your oral health, including which products you should use to take care of your teeth and gums at home.

Does gum disease treatment hurt?

This is a very common question, particularly among patients who struggle with dental-related anxiety. If that’s the case for you, you can have peace of mind knowing that we’ll go above and beyond to make your experience comfortable. That starts with providing you with an overview of the process and extends to numbing the area we’re treating.

Will I eventually lose my teeth with gum disease?

The longer the infection is left alone to progress, the higher the chances are of your teeth falling out. That’s why we advise against taking the “wait and see” approach! Instead, you should call us right away if you notice anything unusual, such as a pimple-like bump on your gums. From there, we can schedule an exam and determine if you’d benefit from gum disease treatment in Texarkana.

Is gum disease considered a dental emergency?

In the early stages, the symptoms of gum disease are relatively minor. That said, the infection can quickly spread, wreaking havoc on your oral and overall health in the process. For that reason, it is considered a dental emergency. So, don’t waste any time contacting us! The sooner you do, the sooner we can provide the care that’s needed to restore your healthy, beautiful smile.
